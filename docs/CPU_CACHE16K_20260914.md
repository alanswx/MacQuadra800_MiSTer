# 16 KB instruction and data caches (2026-09-14, candidate)

Built on the shift-retire candidate (`CPU_SHIFT_RETIRE_20260914.md`) so
the two can be measured against the same base; the cache change is
independent of the core changes and can be committed on its own.

## Why

The checkpoint 8 Speedometer bracket profile (`CPU_DECODE_HISTOGRAM_20260914.md`,
same probe run) puts `S_MRD` at 317 M of 1,094 M cycles (29 %) for 63.0 M
reads, five clocks per read against three for a warm translated hit.
Broken down by the cache state the core waited in:

| S_MRD cycles while the cache was in | cycles | share of bracket |
|---|---:|---:|
| C_IDLE (request, ack, queue holds) | 174.3 M | 15.9 % |
| C_FILL (misses, 3.89 M data fills) | 63.8 M | 5.8 % |
| C_PASS (bypassed: 3.80 M line-crossing reads at 10.5 clocks, 0.47 M other) | 51.4 M | 4.7 % |
| C_LOOK | 24.4 M | 2.2 % |

Stores add 9.8 M cycles of fill wait and the instruction side 1.34 M fills
(about 21 M cycles).  Speedometer's CPU Mix working sets (the 8,191-byte
Sieve flag array, the Bubble Sort array, Dhrystone's records and strings)
do not fit a 4 KB 4-way data cache; a 16 KB cache holds them.  The
MC68040 geometry (64 sets x 4 ways x 16 bytes per bank) is an
implementation choice here, not an architectural one: the caches are
physically indexed and tagged behind the MMU (`wombat_cpu.sv` feeds
`mm_addr`), so a larger index needs no aliasing care, CINV/CPUSH and the
snoop invalidates address rows by physical set, and software cannot
observe the size except through timing.

## What changes

`ap040_cache.v` gains `SETW` (index bits per bank, 6 = 4 KB, 8 = 16 KB)
and derives everything from it: `ROWIW = SETW+1` (tag rows, `{bank,
set}`), `DIDXW = SETW+3` (data words, `{bank, set, way}`), `TAGW = 28 -
SETW` (tag bits above the index, 20 at 16 KB), `ROWW = 2 + 4 + 4*TAGW`.
Every set/row/word index, the snoop compares on `s_addr`, the second-line
invalidate increment, the sweep counter and its bank bit, the idle-hit
shadow indexes and the tag-row slices follow the parameters.  No state,
policy or timing changes: a hit is still a hit in the same cycle, fills
and posted stores are untouched.  The reset and CINV sweeps walk 512 rows
per bank instead of 128 (15 us at 33 MHz; the profile shows `C_SWEEP`
below 0.01 % of bracket cycles).

Storage: the four data arrays go from 512 to 2,048 longwords (2 to 8 M10K
each, +24 M10K; the build had 459 of 553 in use) and the tag RAM from 128
to 512 rows of 86 bits (still 5 M10K in the 512x20 mode).  Only the index
fan-out and the tag compare width (narrower by two bits) touch logic.

Candidate tree `/tmp/dc16-cand.*`; script
`scratchpad/widen_cache.py` (applied to the committed cache, 108 changed
lines).  `tb_ap040_cache_snoop.v` waited 200 clocks for the reset sweep;
its two waits are 600 in the candidate (bench accommodation to the row
count, not a behaviour change).

## Results so far

- AP suite 11 of 11 (after the sweep-wait change; `cache`, `cache_snoop`,
  `mmu` cover fills, snoops, invalidates, CINV and translation).
- Corpus gate: 33,768,178 cycles (+44,928 over the 33,723,250 baseline,
  matching the 100 tests' 384-clock-longer reset sweeps), 0 REAL diffs.
- Handoff latency fixture: 2,300 = 1,916 + the 384-clock sweep.
- Sieve sweep: 422,008 / 410,669 / 519,659 / 434,678 cycles at offsets
  0/6/16/30 against 421,997 / 410,666 / 519,651 / 434,666 (the kernel's
  flags fit the 4 KB cache; identical results, +3 to +12 cycles).
- Boot A/B (first version, one-row sweep): same frame, no faults, data
  fills 1.69 M -> 1.45 M and instruction fills 1.53 M -> 1.31 M, `S_MRD`
  146.4 M -> 140.9 M cycles, but dispatches **75,280,762 -> 74,776,472**
  (-0.7 %): the boot issues 30,690 CINV/CPUSH sweeps and each walked 512
  rows instead of 128 (`C_SWEEP` 4.0 M -> 15.7 M cycles, the core's
  `S_CINV2` wait 15.8 M).  The Speedometer bracket has no sweeps, so the
  benchmark would not have seen it, but Mac OS flushes the caches on every
  code load and a 4x slower CINV is not shippable.

### Two rows per sweep cycle, one bank per CINV (v2)

`scratchpad/sweep2.py` on top of the widening: port A clears the even row
and port B (the snoop/invalidate port) the odd row of each step whenever
port B is not serving a snoop, an owed store invalidate or a
cache-inhibited-hit invalidate; the step repeats until it is, so no row is
skipped.  A snoop whose row is either of the pair is suppressed as before
(both write zero).  The walk starts at the selected bank ({1, 0} for an
instruction-only CINV) and stops at the end of the last selected bank
(`sweep_last`), so a one-bank CINV takes 128 cycles as with the 4 KB
geometry, both banks or reset 256.  v2: AP suite 11/11, corpus
33,738,226 cycles (+14,976: the 128-clock-longer reset sweep, 100 times),
0 REAL diffs.  Boot A/B: **75,584,400 dispatches** (+0.4 % over the
shift candidate's 75,280,762; +0.6 % over checkpoint 10), no faults;
`C_SWEEP` 8.0 M cycles (Mac OS invalidates both banks at once, CINVA BC,
so a sweep is 256 cycles; the 4 KB geometry's was 128).  The frame at the
bracket end differs from every earlier candidate's only in that the second
extension icon has already been drawn: the boot is further along.

`/tmp/MacQuadra800_dc16_v512s20.tezxPW`, 39,168 ALMs (93 %), 479 of 553
M10K (+20 over checkpoint 10's 459; 3,664,262 memory bits), setup slack
clk_sys +0.367 ns, clk_ram +0.472 ns, HDMI +0.365 ns, all TNS zero, no

seed 20 fit placed and then failed in routing ("exceedingly large amount
